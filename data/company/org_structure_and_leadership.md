## How teams are structured

Manna's uses a **three-layer model**:

1. **Function** - the discipline you belong to (for example Engineering). Your
   function owns your career framework, hiring bar and craft standards.
2. **Group** - a durable area of ownership within a function (for example the
   Ingestion Group). Groups own systems and budgets.
3. **Pod** - a cross-functional delivery team of five to nine people, usually one
   engineering lead, three to five engineers, a product manager, a designer and
   a data partner. Pods own outcomes and are re-formed as priorities change.

You will normally have a **functional manager** (who owns your growth, review and
compensation) and a **pod lead** (who owns your day-to-day priorities). When
those two pull in different directions, your functional manager arbitrates.

| Architecture Review | CTO, principal engineers | Cross-cutting technical direction |
| Pricing Committee | CRO, CFO, CPO | Pricing, discounting exceptions |
| Risk & Compliance Board | GC, CFO, CTO, Head of InfoSec | Security posture, audit findings, DPAs |
| Hiring Committee | Function lead + two calibrated interviewers | Offer decisions above grade L5 |

## Escalation path

If something is blocked, escalate in this order and give each step a reasonable
chance to resolve before moving on:

1. Your pod lead
2. Your functional manager
3. Your group lead
4. The function's executive sponsor

For anything involving harassment, discrimination, fraud, or a security breach,
skip the ladder entirely and use the reporting routes in the Code of Conduct and
the Compliance policy. Those routes are designed to bypass your management chain.

## Naming conventions you will hear

- **"The Council"** almost always means Product Council.
- **"AR"** in a calendar invite is Architecture Review, not accounts receivable.
- **"Founders' Review"** is a monthly session where any pod can bring a hard
  trade-off directly to Ananya and Vikram.
